## 1. The one rule that changes our drafts most: implication, not recitation

The trigger's job in copy is CONTEXT-TO-IMPLICATION, never event recap. The prospect LIVED the
event; telling it back to them wastes the only 3-9 seconds they give us (Gong: execs decide to
open in <3s, read <=9s — OBSERVED). The buyer-side proof is brutal: a marketing leader's
instant-delete list includes senders who recap his world back to him wrong or late — fake or
lazy research "is worse than none: it proves the sender didn't look" (Tim Matthews,
goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/10317271-the-six-worst-sdr-emails-i-ve-seen-this-year).
- Braun's move: observation -> "which suggests" -> implication (joshbraun.com/killer-cold-email-opening-lines/).
- Signal-outbound articulation: for a new-exec trigger, don't congratulate — "Most [role] I
  talk to in the first 90 days are looking at two things: [pain 1] and [pain 2]"
  (productstudio.substack.com/p/everyones-doing-outbound-wrong-in).
- SEC filings, req IDs, snapshot dates, "the filing runs one paragraph": OUR evidence-chain
  language. It lives in claims registers and briefs, NEVER in prospect prose. The prospect
  sees only the human-world fact and what it means for THEM.

## 2. Length, form, readability (OBSERVED-DATA, heavily replicated)

- 50-100 words, 3-4 sentences ("the money zone"), no scrolling on mobile. Lavender pushes
  25-50 words for first touch. (gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says;
  lavender.ai/blog/best-length-cold-email; tactics.30mpc.com Ultimate Cold Email Data Report PDF)
- 3rd-5th grade reading level = +67% replies; 70% of cold email is written above 10th grade.
  Short/mobile-clean = +83% replies; first open is 8x more likely on a phone.
  (lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-101; lavender.ai/blog/mobile-matters)
- Each long sentence cuts response odds ~17% (Will Allred, Lavender data, via Exit Five
  podcast: exitfive.com/podcast/cold-email-lessons-from-real-outbound-campaigns-in-2025/).
- Plain text only. HTML/images route to promotions/spam (Allred, same episode).
- CONSEQUENCE FOR US: our current four-layer drafts run ~120-150 words in 4 paragraphs.
  The four layers stay (strategy §5.2) but each layer compresses to roughly ONE sentence.
  Target: 60-90 words, readable on one phone screen without scrolling.

## 3. Subject lines for exec inboxes (OBSERVED-DATA)

- 1-4 words, concrete, internal-sounding, boring: "trial delays", "hiring ops", "RFP Response",
  "Pipeline Issue", "Update forecast" ("Internal Camo" — Jen Allen-Knuth,
  learn.g2.com/industry-insights-jen-allen-knuth-outbound-messaging; Allred; 85M report).
- Question marks -56% opens; numbers -46%; punctuation -36%; recipient's first name -12%
  replies; buzzwords/salesy language up to -17.9% opens. Empty subject = clickbait effect
  (+30% opens, -12% replies) — banned. (lavender.ai/blog/cold-email-subject-line-tips;
  30mpc.com/newsletter/4-data-backed-subject-lines-to-get-your-cold-emails-opened)
- Casing CONFLICT, recorded honestly: Lavender 2023 says title case; the larger, newer 85M
  dataset (2025) says lowercase wins. WE USE lowercase (newer + larger dataset; also matches
  the internal-camo logic).
- Preview text is the second subject line: the first sentence must earn the open too.

## 4. Openers and personalization for director+ (OBSERVED-DATA)

- Gong classifier study (30K+ prospecting emails): activity/company-based personalization = 3x
  replies AND 3x meetings for executives; personal trivia (alma mater, hobbies) only helps
  junior buyers. "Executives don't care if you reference their alma mater."
  (gong.io/blog/4-data-backed-ways-to-increase-your-email-reply-rate-and-book-that-meeting)
- 87% of buyers say sales emails don't address relevant challenges (Gong survey, same URL).
  Relevance (right signal, right moment) IS the personalization; decoration is noise.
- "Informative" lecturing tone = -26% replies (Lavender). Don't educate a CMO about marketing.
- You-language over we-language: prospect-focused pronoun density is a measured winner
  (85M report). Corpus agrees: "we/our/I"-dominated copy alienates (Allen-Knuth).
- No-signal fallback (bench/second-wave touches): cohort relevance — "When I talk to [role] at
  [segment], they tend to be struggling with..." (lavender.ai/blog/personalization-framework-2).

## 5. Proof and metrics — the credibility trap for OUR persona (CRITICAL)

- Pitching in email 1 cuts replies up to -57%; ROI-language -15% (Gong 85M;
  gong.io/blog/cold-email-stats). One problem-framed proof line = +41% (Jason Bay on 85M,
  30mpc.com/newsletter/the-data-backed-cold-email-formula-the-exact-words-length).
- Proof arrives THIRD (after observation and problem), as one specific clause, then status
  returns to the reader with a question (Braun's sentence-3 rule; Lavender framework).
- BUYER-SIDE REALITY (Wynter, n=100 + n=101, wynter.com/post/how-b2b-saas-marketing-leaders-buy-2024
  + wynter.com/cmo-b2b-saas-buyer-journey-report): cold outreach is the second-least-influential
  touchpoint these execs have (2.25/lowest tier); 72% of CMOs check with peers first; 97%
  self-verify on the vendor site. A claim's job is to SURVIVE THE PEER-REPEAT and the website
  check, not to impress in-line. The buyer's stated bar: "something I can't get on my own...
  If you have some real data to offer, then by all means, share it" (Matthews).
- CONSEQUENCE FOR US: one figure per touch stands (corpus, binding) — AND the figure should be
  the most VERIFIABLE one available (published case studies: Todyl, AWS OpenSearch) over
  unnamed-program figures whenever the persona is senior; unnamed figures read as unverifiable
  hype to people who run attribution for a living. Percent-lift phrasing ("185 percent")
  is ROI-language — prefer the concrete outcome told as a fact of the program.

## 6. CTAs and sequence mechanics (OBSERVED-DATA)

- CTA ladder for cold: compelling OFFER (+28%) > interest question > question CTA > meeting
  ask (Gong 304K study; 85M report; Gong 2026 exec study). Our artifact soft-ask IS the offer
  CTA — doctrine validated; keep artifacts real, specific, immediately buildable
  (meeting-corpus §4.5 already binds this).
- Tentative/unsure tone measurably wins with execs: "Realize I may be off, but is this a
  priority?" (+35% replies claim, Lavender; "triggers the human urge to correct",
  Allen-Knuth). ADD to our ask-layer toolbox.
- Micro-friction permission CTA: "Want me to send it over?" (ours) matches the pattern
  ("Can I send you a 2min video?" — clay.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-copywriting).
- Sequences: 70% of replies come AFTER email 1; ~6 touches over 14-28 days, reply/send falls
  below 0.5% past that (85M). In-thread bumps = 1-2 sentences; breakup language nearly
  doubles bump replies (+89%); asking someone to go look at a case study in a bump = -47%
  (30mpc.com/newsletter/the-data-backed-blueprint-for-multi-touch-prospecting).
- Calls layered on email double-to-triple email replies (85M) — our SDR call pointers already
  pair; add the voicemail-as-email-nudge move where dialing exists.
- Follow-up length CONFLICT recorded: Gong 2021 (304K) favored 30-150-word follow-ups; 85M
  (2025) favors 1-2-sentence bumps. Resolution the sources imply: SAME-THREAD bumps short;
  a NEW-ANGLE follow-up (our email 2) carries its own context and can run full form.

## 7. Guilt mechanics and banned phrases (buyer-voice + data, ADD to banned list)

Instant deletes for a marketing exec (Matthews, first-person; Gong numbers where present):
- "Just bumping this" / "I never heard back" (-14%) — reframes the seller as accuser.
- "Is something wrong?" subject, "final email" ultimatums — guilt theater.
- "Thoughts?" as a bump (-20% meetings, Gong; decaying fast per Tatulea).
- Congrats-flattery openers, pitching a goal they already achieved, pitching a product they
  already use — half-researched worse than unresearched.
- Anything that "screams marketing": long clever subjects, emoji, HTML.
- NOTE an honest data quirk: "Hope all is well" measured +24% in Gong's 2021 set — but our
  corpus strip-filler rule and three practitioner sources ban greeting filler; the ban stands
  (the 2021 correlation is dated, single-set, and contradicts the buyer-voice evidence).

## 8. Reconciliation with our binding corpus (authority order applied)

UNCHANGED AND REINFORCED by this research:
- Four-layer INSIGHT/PROBLEM/PROOF/SOFT-ASK order (= observation/problem/proof-third/offer-CTA
  — externally validated at every layer). Layer EXECUTION changes: see §1 (insight =
  implication) and §2 (one sentence per layer).
- One figure per touch; one number set per account (corpus, on OUR tape) — reinforced by
  -57% pitch and -15% ROI-language findings.
- Artifact does the selling; no meeting ask on first touch — reinforced (+28% offer CTA).
- Bare LinkedIn connect note — untouched.
- No em dashes / AI-tell stripping — reinforced: in the AI-slop inbox, human plain-text is
  the arbitrage. Lavender Feb-2026: only ~6% of emails grade A, and A-grade earns +27-79%
  reply lift while the average rep burns 344 sends per meeting. Craft compounds, volume died.
- Claims register discipline — reinforced by the peer-repeat test (§5).

CHANGED (this file governs, copy-rules.md §Email-craft pointer added 2026-08-20):
1. INSIGHT layer = the trigger's IMPLICATION in the prospect's world; the event itself gets at
   most a subordinate clause; system/evidence language (filings, req numbers, snapshot dates)
   banned in prospect prose.
2. Length: 60-90 words target, 3-5 sentences, one phone screen, no scrolling; reading level
   grade 5-ish.
3. Subjects: 1-3 lowercase internal-camo words; no punctuation, numbers, names, questions.
4. Ask layer gains the tentative-tone option ("Realize I may be off — is this a priority right
   now?") alongside the artifact offer.
5. Proof preference: most-verifiable figure available (published > unnamed); no percent-lift
   ROI phrasing to senior marketing personas.
6. Bump/breakup rules: in-thread bumps 1-2 sentences; breakup keeps the alternative close but
   NEVER asks them to go read a case study.
7. Banned-phrase list extended per §7.
8. Write for the forward: 79% of CMOs run team evaluations (Wynter) — every decision-maker
   touch must survive being forwarded to the delegate who actually evaluates ("who owns
   this?" is a WIN outcome; make the email brief that person too).
