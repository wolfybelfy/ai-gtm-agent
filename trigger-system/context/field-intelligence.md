# Field Intelligence

This is the sanitized operating memory for play generation. It distills what happened in real outbound calls and discovery meetings without carrying contact-level data into Git. It is evidence for judgment, not permission to state a company-specific pain as fact.

## Corpus and coverage limitations

- The first JustCall synthesis covers **72 analyzed calls** from the May corpus. Fifty-six contained substantive prospect interaction.
- The Q2 synthesis covers 316 calls from March through May and is strongest on positioning consistency, contact freshness, discovery discipline, and referral handling.
- The newer July-August synthesis covers **123 analyzed calls** from 249 eligible recordings and 920 coded signals across seven SDRs. The 60-second floor excluded 126 calls, including 34 callback requests. August coverage ends on August 21, 2026. Missing dispositions and unresolved identities mean absence is never proof that a pattern did not occur.
- The meeting corpus contains the original discovery and demo set plus the later SCC meeting. It is qualitative and directional, not a controlled experiment.
- Several enriched accounts were agencies. Do not generalize an agency objection pattern to every end buyer.
- Raw recordings and transcripts remain outside this repository. This file contains no contact names, personal email addresses, or phone numbers.

## What prospects repeatedly cared about

### Capacity and priority collision

The most common real constraint was not ignorance of the marketing problem. It was lack of bandwidth while several initiatives competed for the same team. Hiring bursts, open ownership gaps, integration work, and leadership resets make this pressure more credible. A play should describe the likely trade-off created by the trigger, not claim the prospect is understaffed.

### Structural change resets plans

New leaders, acquisitions, rebrands, regional expansion, and annual planning can reopen audience, channel, measurement, and agency choices. The useful question is what the change forces the team to decide now. Congratulations language adds little.

### Audience quality before volume

Prospects challenged broad claims and wanted confidence that the seller understood the buyers and industries that mattered. Representative language included “everything you said is kind of very broad” and “leads aren’t a KPI.” A volume pitch without audience alignment increases distrust.

### Pipeline and lead quality are joined

Real conversations did not treat lead quality as a standalone top-of-funnel issue. Prospects connected it to lower-funnel progression, sales acceptance, the definition of success, and whether the program could influence measurable pipeline.

### Planning windows are actionable

The newer corpus surfaced a planning window in April, October, November, and year-end conversations. Treat an observed planning window as a clock. Do not infer a budget month for another company from these examples.

### Ownership is often unclear or elsewhere

“Someone else owns this” and remit mismatch were common. The correct move is to identify the actual owner and equip the current contact to route the idea. A vague referral request underperformed a precise explanation of who should care and why.

## Objection hierarchy from the newer calls

The largest observed clusters were immediate timing, another owner, confidence in an internal team, planning-cycle timing, remit mismatch, questions about the source of contact data, existing agencies, committed or unavailable budget, and do-not-contact requests.

Operational consequences:

- Timing: tie the play to a verified clock and offer a specific re-entry point only when the prospect gives one.
- Authority: stop pitching, learn the owner, and give the contact a forwardable business reason.
- In-house or agency: do not invent a replacement narrative. Frame a narrow capacity or specialist gap only when the evidence supports it.
- Data-source question: answer identity and sourcing plainly. Evasion destroys trust.
- Do not contact: acknowledge and suppress. No salvage attempt.

## What advanced conversations

- Discovery tied to a visible gap before proof.
- A two-option pain question when both options were grounded in the trigger.
- A senior colleague attached to the next meeting when their role was clear.
- A placeholder invite paired with explicit acceptance, not an assumed booking.
- The prospect defining success criteria in their own language.
- A narrow case study used after relevance was established.
- A concrete, already-available artifact that helped a champion explain the issue internally.
- Clear referral routing and correct employment data.

## What damaged conversations

- Long service tours before discovery.
- Client-logo recitation without the outcome or why it matched.
- Case studies with unsupported figures.
- Calling the company a platform, database, or list.
- Broad claims that did not show industry or audience understanding.
- Continuing through an authority, timing, relevance, or do-not-contact objection.
- Wrong names, stale contacts, unclear identity, missing promised materials, or a seller who could not explain their own terminology.
- Zero-discovery meetings and long monologues.

## Meeting-stage lessons that affect first touch

The strongest discovery calls used an audience-alignment question, started with the prospect's operating reality, and translated the problem into a small diagnostic next step. The useful narrative worked backwards from revenue, audience, channel arithmetic, lead processing, and committee nurture. It should inform thinking, not be pasted into an email.

An insider detail can open a door when it is genuinely relevant, but trivia reads as surveillance. A champion needs language that can travel to the budget owner and adjacent operators. The first email should therefore be forwardable without pretending that the recipient is the sole buyer.

## Buying-committee inference

Infer roles from the work created by the trigger:

- The person living the operational pain may become a champion.
- The person accountable for the business result may be a decision-maker.
- Owners of adjacent systems, data, brand, sales, finance, or regional execution may influence the decision.
- An incumbent team or agency can be a blocker, partner, or neither; do not label it without evidence.
- A connector or insider is useful only when there is a credible routing reason.

These are optional functional labels, not a required triad or a contact-count target. Separate the full committee hypothesis from the smaller outreach subset.

## Language and positioning

Use plain business language. Lead with the consequence of the verified change, then a carefully graded hypothesis. Pick one capability. Use one matched proof point only when it is currently approved and survives lookup. Otherwise write without a number.

The safe identity is an embedded B2B agency partner that helps teams identify the right buyers, influence them earlier, and connect attention to measurable pipeline. “Brand and Demand” and “brand-to-revenue” are the approved formulations. ABM is appropriate in external copy; ABX is internal terminology unless the prospect uses it.

## Contradictions and safety rulings

- All audience-size figures are blocked even when they appear in recordings.
- Client claims made on calls are not approved merely because a leader said them.
- Internal pricing is never quoted from the corpus.
- Event proof is not reused for rebrand, paid-media, or pipeline plays; pipeline proof is not reused as webinar proof.
- A claim with an expired review date, unresolved identity, missing public source, or conflicting number set is omitted.
- The newer call evidence supersedes the older memory where it adds coverage, but it does not erase documented contradictions.
