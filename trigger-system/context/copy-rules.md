# Copy rules (strategy §5.2 + §4 + plan v1.1/v1.2 — binding for every prospect-facing draft)

## Structure — four layers, in order, every time (strategy §5.2 / transcript #10)
1. **INSIGHT** — something true and specific we found, with its evidence URL on file ("24 open roles, none in marketing, while launching in 30 countries").
2. **PROBLEM** — what usually breaks in exactly this situation; we name it.
3. **PROOF** — one line, not a stat dump. Only claims cleared by `context\offer.md` + the conflict rules.
4. **SOFT ASK** — a specific useful artifact, never a meeting ("Want me to send it over?"). The artifact does the qualifying.

## Hard rules
- Never a meeting ask on the first touch. Never "saw you went to Harvard"-class personalization.
- Every claim traces to evidence fetched fresh; if the why-now reads generic, the play is DOWNGRADED, not decorated with adjectives.
- Facts stated as facts; inferences as hypotheses; negative evidence carries its coverage limitation ("careers page checked <date>: no marketing roles visible" — not "they have no marketers").
- **No surveillance language, ever** (deviation (c)): "you asked for the checklist" — fine; "I saw you read page 3" — banned.
- Strip every AI formatting tell mechanically before human review: em dashes, headers, bullet symmetry, "I hope this finds you well"-class filler (strategy §5.2.3).
- 3-email sequence + LinkedIn connect note (NO pitch in the connect note — bare connects converted best) + 3 short subject variants.
- Every send is the OWNER's action, and the run is recorded: `RUN_BY:` / `RUN_DATE:` in the play's status.md (owner-run model, user directive 2026-08-16 — no approval workflow exists). The record is a log, not a gate; the discipline that never bends is that a human does the sending and the record gets written.

## Voice of customer (mandatory read before drafting)

approved vs banned claims (2M community NOT "100 million"; only the approved AWS OpenSearch

## Meeting-layer rules (added 2026-08-14 from the SD meeting corpus; sections cited)
- **One figure per touch; one number set per account.** Stat recitation was flat or harmful on
  tape (call-openers §4); different Unbound people quoting different numbers on one account made
  prospects doubt both (meeting-corpus §5). The play's claims register is the account's single
  number set.
- **The artifact does the selling** (meeting-corpus §4.5): the soft-ask artifact must be real,
  specific, and buildable the moment the owner runs the play; the first meeting opens with the audience-alignment count
  (their segments, our audience) — the pattern that turned a cancelled meeting into a champion
  (§4.1).
- **Audience-size figures stay BLOCKED in copy** (offer.md top block) even though leadership uses
  them live on calls — until the user rules on the taxonomy (meeting-corpus §2 flag). The 86K+
  figure is a campaign-audience count and is usable; community/database sizes are not.
- **Reframe before proof** (meeting-corpus §4.3): assume the prospect thinks we are a platform or
  a syndication list; the first line of any call answers what we actually are. BANT is jargon to
  marketers — explain the gates in plain words when qualification comes up.

## Email-craft layer (added 2026-08-20 — external research run, user-ordered)
`context\email-craft-insights.md` is MANDATORY READ alongside this file for every email draft.
It binds (its §8 lists the full set): the INSIGHT layer states the trigger's IMPLICATION, never
recites the event (system/evidence language — filings, req numbers, snapshot dates — banned in
prospect prose); 60-90 words, 3-5 sentences, one phone screen; subjects 1-3 lowercase
internal-camo words; tentative-tone ask option; most-verifiable figure preferred, no
percent-lift ROI phrasing to senior marketing personas; in-thread bumps 1-2 sentences, breakup
never asks them to read a case study; extended banned-phrase list (guilt mechanics,
"Thoughts?", congrats-flattery). Where that file and the call corpus conflict, the corpus wins
on claims and call behavior; the data wins on email mechanics.
