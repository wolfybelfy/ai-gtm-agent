## 1. Why TradeStation, and why now — two real triggers

**[VERIFIED]** **TradeStation Europe B.V. launched 10 June 2026** — a fully licensed MiFID Investment Firm, regulated by the Dutch AFM, headquartered in Amsterdam, live across all 30 EEA countries. It gives European retail and institutional investors the same US equities/options/futures access TradeStation Securities offers domestically.

**[VERIFIED]** In 2026, **Tom Hammergren** moved from Chief Customer Officer to a newly created role: **Chief Growth Officer, TradeStation Group & TradeStation Securities.** TradeStation's public leadership page lists no separate CMO — Hammergren is the single named executive over growth/marketing spend.

**[OBSERVED]** His own About section states the mandate explicitly:
> *"I own the end-to-end customer acquisition, activation, and revenue growth engine across retail, institutional, and partnership channels — connecting Marketing, Sales, Business Development, Product, and Technology to turn strategic positioning into measurable outcomes."*
> *"…now with a forward focus on building TradeStation's growth model for the emerging AI-agent era in financial services."*

**[HYPOTHESIS], grounded in the two verified events:** a brand-new market (Europe, a different regulatory and buyer context) plus a brand-new growth mandate (explicitly unifying previously separate functions) together read as a company building new demand infrastructure, not running its existing one on autopilot. That combination — new market + new growth leadership — is the trigger, not TradeStation-the-brand generically.

**Fit = MED–HIGH** for the Acquisition Marketing function specifically. There is no "big logo trap" question here the way there was with ServiceNow (TradeStation is ~500–1,000 employees, not an enterprise giant with its own agency-scale in-house machine) — but the same discipline applies: resolve to the actual function that owns growth spend, not the brand.

It does **not** establish budget, timing, an active RFP, or that TradeStation is looking for an agency. It is a worldview/priority signal, exactly like the Armis example: it hands us her language and the exact gap she's naming, not a buying intent.

## 6–8. Cadence, qualification, and pilot measurement

Unchanged from the operating model established for the portfolio play: Day 1 email+call → Day 3 LinkedIn touch → Day 5 second call+voicemail → Day 7 proof-led email → Day 10 second stakeholder (e.g., Hammergren, carefully) → Day 12 final problem-focused touch → qualify/refer/nurture/close. First two weeks measure discovery yield before any quota. No reply-rate, meeting-rate, or pipeline forecast is invented.

---

## What this run proved

1. **The named decision-maker (Hammergren) has zero public signal.** The real, usable disclosure came one level down, from the function director who actually owns acquisition spend — reinforcing that the watchlist must include the operators, not just the C-suite name.
2. **A real, well-articulated signal can still be the wrong buying center.** Stephanie Dempster's AI-governance post was genuine and specific — and correctly excluded, because it belongs to Risk/Compliance, not Marketing. Discipline here means not treating "found a real quote" as license to force a fit.
3. **The Robledo quote is an industry observation, not a confirmed internal TradeStation problem** — the confidence score (≈60) and unknowns list reflect that honestly; it is weaker evidence than Armis's Lizi de Jong Reuveni post, which named specific internal operating failures. This one names a *pattern across the industry* she's observed, which is suggestive but not proof TradeStation itself has the gap.
