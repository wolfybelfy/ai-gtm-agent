The plan, explained simply

A walkthrough of the trigger-based outbound system. Meant to be read and discussed, not filed away.

At any moment, only about 5% of a market is ready to buy. This system exists to find that 5% inside accounts we already chose — and get there while the door is open.
01

What's broken today

Outbound as most teams run it: build a big list, write a decent template, send a lot of email, hope.

Reply rates keep falling because every inbox on earth gets the same thing, often written by the same AI tools. And blasting the 95% who aren't ready doesn't just waste effort — it burns the domain and the brand for the day they are ready.

100 buyers in a market. The orange five are ready right now. Everyone's template blast goes to all 100. Our system only spends effort where it's orange — and knows why it's orange.

Everything below is machinery for one sentence:

Reach the right team, for a real reason, while the window is still open.
02

The two ideas everything sits on

Teams, not companies

Our accounts are big. At a company with thousands of people, “VP Marketing” exists many times — one per product line, per region, per acquired brand, each with their own budget and problems. An email aimed at “ServiceNow” lands nowhere. An email aimed at the Armis marketing team, months after the acquisition closed, about the exact mess acquisitions create — lands.

25 accounts → 100+ real targets. Not extra work — the actual market becoming visible.

Timing is detectable

People hire an agency when something just changed: a new boss, a market launch, a merger, a big event coming up, goals that outgrew the team. Almost all of these changes leave public traces, and most traces are free to watch daily.

So we don't guess who's ready. We know, with a date on it — and just as important, we know when a window has closed, so nobody wastes a week on a dead one.

The windows we spot for later (an event in November, a platform launch “coming months”) go on a reminder calendar. That calendar compounds: every future window is a meeting attempt already scheduled.

04

The honesty rules

1One trigger alone rarely sends an email

Two independent triggers on the same team, both in-window, is the bar for a real play. One trigger = sequence quietly, or watch with a reminder date.

2Every claim traces to evidence

A quote from their job post, a date from their press release. If the “why now” reads generic, the play gets downgraded — not decorated.

3A human approves every send

The machine researches, drafts, and queues. A person reads and clicks. Nothing reaches a prospect on autopilot.

05 · The proof

A real example, run by hand yesterday

We ran one account through the whole thing manually — zero paid tools, about 35 minutes: TradeStation.

  • On June 10 they launched a whole European company: Amsterdam entity, licensed for 30 countries, brand-new president (Peter Comstock).
  • Their careers page, checked live: 24 open jobs — not one in marketing. Launching in 30 countries with no visible demand team.
  • Comstock is ~7 weeks into the job — right inside the new-leader window.
Verdict: strike now, on the Europe team specifically — window closes ~December. Three emails already drafted, offering a short playbook on how US trading firms built European demand in year one. Soft ask, useful thing, tracked link.
JAN 6New flagship platform announced — full launch “coming months.” Window not open yet → on the reminder calendar.
JUN 10Europe launch + new president → two live triggers on one team.
JUL 27Careers page checked live: zero marketing hires for a 30-country launch. That line goes in the email.
~DECExpansion window closes. After that, this play is worth nothing — which is exactly why timing is the whole system.

One account, one sitting, no tools. The daily system does this across every account, every morning — and the US team's “watch” reminder fires automatically the day their platform launch is announced.

06

What the emails look like

Never “saw you went to Harvard.” Never a meeting ask on the first touch. Four layers, in order, every time:

1 · INSIGHTSomething true and specific we found: “24 open roles, none in marketing, while launching in 30 countries.”
2 · PROBLEMWhat usually breaks in exactly this situation — we've seen it before, we name it.
3 · PROOFOne line. Not a stat dump — just enough to show we've done this.
4 · SOFT ASK“Want me to send it over?” A useful artifact, not a calendar link. Low friction to say yes.

Why the soft ask: saying “yes, send it” is easy. Then opening and reading it takes real effort — and whoever spends that effort has qualified themselves. That's when the phone call happens, and it's a warm one.

The actual TradeStation drafts, trimmed

Email 1 · day 0 · subj: year one in Europe

Peter — congrats on the June 10 launch. An AFM license covering 30 EEA markets on day one is a serious footprint.

The pattern when US platforms enter Europe: the product lands months before the demand engine does — your careers page shows 24 open roles and zero marketing seats.

We wrote up how the last wave of US trading platforms handled EEA year one — the three mistakes and the 90-day sequence that avoids them. Want me to send it over?

Email 2 · +4 days · subj: cold-start pipeline

Peter — one datapoint from the playbook I mentioned: the platforms that won early EEA pipeline sequenced institutional demand before retail brand spend — the reverse of their US playbook.

It's a 10-minute read. Worth a look before Q4 planning locks?

Email 3 · +9 days · subj: timing

Peter — if the European demand engine is already handled internally, ignore me with a clear conscience.

If it's still on the “after we hire” list, the playbook is yours either way — it's the thinking we'd bring, free to steal. One word and I'll send it.

Every claim in email 1 traces to something verified that morning. No meeting ask anywhere — the playbook does the asking.
07

What the SDR sees (no new tools)

Claude Code watches and writes. Clay finds and verifies people. HubSpot is where everything lands. An SDR's morning:

  • Slack alert: “Strike: TradeStation Europe. New market launch + new president, window closes December. Accept within 24h.”
  • The HubSpot record already holds everything: the why-now paragraph, evidence links, the 5–8 right people, drafted emails as a note. Review, tweak, approve, go.
  • Someone replies or opens the asset → “call this person now, here's the number, here's what they just read.”

The SDR does none of the research and all of the selling. The machine watches, drafts, and alerts; the human approves every send, makes every call, and owns the fast-reply clock.

09

What it costs

Almost the whole watching-and-thinking layer is free: public job feeds, press releases, event exhibitor lists, Claude Code we already pay for, Slack webhooks.

Paid parts, all capped: Clay credits only for contact-finding on active plays (tens of dollars per batch, not hundreds) · a sending tool in the $100–200/month range plus a few extra domains · optionally a small LinkedIn ads budget aimed only at accounts we're actively striking — so the people we're emailing also see our proof content that month. Sales and marketing pointed at the same accounts, on purpose.

10

Four decisions needed — none slow the build

  1. 1 · Conflict lines. Which companies are off-limits because of current client relationships. Sign-off before anything sends — one wrong email can cost a whole logo.
  2. 2 · Sending setup. Does our HubSpot tier include sequences, or do we buy a sender? Either way, new domains get bought day one — warming takes 2–3 weeks.
  3. 3 · Europe. Legal basis per region before we email anyone in the EU. The TradeStation play may literally depend on this.
  4. 4 · Who owns the alerts. A named person on the strike queue and the fast-reply clock. Alerts into an empty room book nothing.
11

What could go wrong, said out loud

The triggers could be wrong for our market. That's why weights start as guesses, get backtested against our own wins and losses in week 2, and get killed by the weekly numbers. The system is built to be wrong quickly and cheaply.

Every number in the source videos and newsletters is self-reported by someone selling something. We copied their mechanics, not their claims. Our bar is our own baseline times three — not somebody's YouTube number.

Volume will feel low at first. That's the design. Ten sharp plays a week beat a thousand sprays, and the watch list compounds: every future window we spot is a meeting attempt already scheduled.

It needs the manual reps first. Weeks 1 and 2 are deliberately hands-on. Automating an unproven motion just ships mistakes faster.
