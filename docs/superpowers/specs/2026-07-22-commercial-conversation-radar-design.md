# VP-Level Buyer-Problem Outreach Presentation Design

## Purpose

Explain the strategy from a VP Marketing and VP Strategy perspective. The document is a working commercial strategy, not a product description or software specification.

## Strategic premise

ICP fit tells Unbound IA which accounts and buyers are relevant. It does not tell us what those buyers are dealing with now.

LinkedIn comments can reveal a current operating issue in the buyer's own words. The comment may describe pipeline pressure, unclear marketing contribution, budget constraints, weak account movement, operational friction or demand that is not converting.

The strategy is to identify those disclosures, confirm the target account and buyer relevance, validate the issue against wider business context, then use it to write a more relevant email with the matching Unbound IA proof.

## Main VP narrative

1. Five strategic decisions: find the right conversation, identify a possible issue, confirm account and buyer relevance, match the issue to proof and message, then measure whether relevance converts.
2. Business problems worth monitoring.
3. Real screenshots showing the difference between engagement and a substantive disclosure.
4. Account, role and context checks that establish commercial relevance.
5. A Databricks walkthrough showing how an attribution problem maps to the AWS OpenSearch email.
6. The approved proof and two email versions.
7. The commercial assumptions and first test.
8. Areas where VP perspective changes the strategy.

## Execution appendix

Detailed Google queries, 40+ and 100+ starting thresholds, SERP fields, the spare-PC flow and the daily cadence appear after the VP discussion. They support the strategy without dominating it.

## Content rules

- Use can reveal and may describe until account and context checks confirm the issue.
- Use pain point where it clarifies the idea, then vary with business issue, operating problem, pressure and what the team is facing.
- Keep all three screenshots uncropped.
- Keep Databricks grounded in the user-provided account list and label the comment wording illustrative.
- Keep AWS OpenSearch, both email drafts, Todyl and the exact Selected proof / only approved claims label.
- Do not invent a person, business situation, buying intent or outcome.
- Do not include product-launch language, ethics framing, THE ASK or a final CTA.

## Design direction

Retain the editorial working-paper format, navigation rail, paper-and-ink palette, copper accents and restrained diagrams.

The main process uses five executive decisions. The Databricks section includes a three-stage issue-to-email panel:

- What their comment reveals
- What pain it points to
- How the email uses it

The panel collapses to one column below 1180px.

## Acceptance criteria

- The opening says comments can reveal pain points and emails can use the validated issue.
- The main narrative is strategy-led; detailed search and scheduling are in an appendix.
- The Databricks issue, AWS proof and email angle are aligned around marketing contribution and opportunity progression.
- Business caveats appear as uncertainty in the logic, not compliance language.
- The commercial test compares issue-led outreach with ICP-only outreach.
- The final section requests VP perspective on choices, not approval.
