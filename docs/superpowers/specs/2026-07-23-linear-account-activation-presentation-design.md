# Linear Account Activation Presentation Design

## Purpose

Rebuild the HTML presentation as a practical VP-level strategy for turning relevant LinkedIn discussion into account intelligence, SDR action, qualified conversations and learning.

The presentation explains a strategy. It does not present a product launch, a software feature set or a promise that one personalized email will convert an enterprise prospect.

## Strategic correction

A LinkedIn comment is an early signal, not a lead and not buying intent.

The comment may reveal:

- a business problem in the buyer's own language;
- a current pressure, constraint or disagreement;
- a topic the person cares enough about to discuss publicly.

The signal becomes commercially useful only after Unbound IA:

1. verifies the current account and role;
2. checks whether the company is on the target-account list;
3. checks CRM ownership, history and active opportunities;
4. looks for supporting account context;
5. prepares an SDR-ready brief;
6. works the account through several relevant touches;
7. qualifies a response before creating an opportunity;
8. recycles or nurtures the account when timing is not ready.

## Evidence boundary

The presentation uses four evidence types and labels them clearly.

### Observed evidence

The three supplied LinkedIn screenshots are shown uncropped. Exact comments may be quoted because they are visible in the supplied evidence.

The clearest observed example is Amy Spencer's comment:

> "We've over-indexed on where a deal started instead of what helped it move and close. Deals are built across dozens of touchpoints."

This is evidence of a marketing-contribution problem being discussed. It does not establish purchase intent, budget, timing or target-account status.

### Verified platform capability

Clay documentation supports:

- company and person enrichment;
- work-email and phone waterfalls;
- Salesforce and HubSpot lookup and update workflows;
- routing to Outreach and Salesloft sequences;

### Verified Unbound IA proof

The AWS OpenSearch case study supports an always-on, multi-touch demand programme with targeting, content, events, nurture, retargeting, sales enablement and SDR call scripts. Its published metrics may be used only as stated by Unbound IA.

### Illustrative operating example

The end-to-end account activation walkthrough uses a fictional company named Northstar Cloud. It is labelled illustrative throughout. The account, person, CRM record, phone number, buying group and sales outcome are not presented as real.

No Databricks example appears anywhere in the presentation.

## Complete operating model

The presentation shows this as one vertical spine:

```text
Relevant discussion found
↓
Substantive comment captured
↓
Current company resolved
↓
Target-account and role fit checked
↓
CRM history and ownership checked
↓
Clay enrichment and supporting research
↓
SDR accepts or rejects the lead
↓
Email, call and LinkedIn sequence
↓
Reply and qualification
↓
Account director handoff
↓
Opportunity, nurture or close
↓
Outcome improves the next signal decision
```

## Clay's role

Clay is the data and orchestration layer. It is not the entire strategy and it does not replace the SDR.

The page explains Clay in six vertical stages:

1. Receive the raw post, comment and profile data.
2. Resolve the company and current role.
3. Look up the CRM record before creating a new record.
4. Enrich email, phone and account context.
5. prepare a concise SDR brief and suggested channel copy.
6. Upsert the CRM record and route the approved lead to the sales-engagement cadence.

If Unbound IA does not yet have a fixed CRM or sequencer, the page names the layer generically and gives Salesforce, HubSpot, Outreach and Salesloft as documented examples.

## SDR operating model

The SDR receives a compact record containing:

- observed comment;
- problem hypothesis;
- evidence strength;
- account fit;
- current role;
- CRM owner and history;
- work email and phone;
- supporting company context;
- relevant Unbound IA proof;
- suggested first email;
- suggested call opener;
- recommended next task;
- rejection and recycle options.

The example cadence is a pilot design, not a universal benchmark:

1. Day 1: personalized email and first call.
2. Day 3: LinkedIn connection or relevant engagement.
3. Day 5: second call and short voicemail.
4. Day 7: proof-led email.
5. Day 10: account-level follow-up or carefully selected second stakeholder.
6. Day 12: final problem-focused email and call.
7. After the sequence: qualify, refer, nurture, monitor or close.

Every touch has a different job. The sequence does not repeat a Calendly link without adding new value.

## Qualification and handoff

A response is not automatically an opportunity.

The SDR confirms:

- whether the issue is current;
- whether there is an active initiative;
- whether the person owns or influences it;
- what the current approach is;
- whether another stakeholder should be involved;
- whether a deeper working conversation is justified.

The account director or sales lead takes over after a qualified meeting. The CRM outcome is one of:

- opportunity created;
- nurture with a defined next date;
- referred to another person;
- no current initiative;
- wrong account or role;
- no response after the agreed sequence.

## Pilot

The first two weeks measure discovery yield before an outreach quota is set.

The pilot then uses:

- a small named-account panel;
- one or two pain themes;
- manual review of every activated record;
- a fixed SDR capacity;
- one approved multi-channel sequence;
- one account-fit-only comparison group;
- CRM dispositions on every record.

The pilot reports:

- useful signals per 100 substantive comments reviewed;
- target-account matches;
- Clay enrichment coverage;
- SDR acceptance;
- time from signal to first touch;
- calls connected;
- positive replies;
- meetings held;
- qualified opportunities;
- nurture and rejection reasons;
- performance by pain theme, proof and channel.

The presentation does not invent a reply-rate, meeting-rate or pipeline forecast.

## Visual direction

### Subject and audience

The subject is the internal movement of one account signal through Unbound IA. The audience is a VP Marketing or VP Strategy director. The page's job is to make ownership, evidence and next action understandable while the presenter scrolls downward.

### Layout

- One centered reading column with a maximum width near 980px.
- No fixed side rail.
- No horizontal flowcharts.
- No wide comparison tables.
- Every process is a vertical list connected by one continuous line.
- Screenshots appear full width and uncropped.
- Proof, email and call examples appear one after another.
- Appendices remain in the same vertical reading path.

### Token system

- Ink: `#17212B`
- Paper: `#F6F7F4`
- Signal blue: `#245B78`
- Verified green: `#2F6B57`
- Review amber: `#B16A2B`
- Rule: `#CBD2D0`
- White: `#FFFFFF`

Typography:

- Display: Fraunces for restrained editorial emphasis.
- Body: Manrope for clear presentation copy.
- Utility: DM Mono for evidence labels, CRM fields and search queries.

### Signature

One vertical signal spine runs through the page. At each stage, the record gains a new verified field. The visual reinforces the strategic point that a raw comment becomes useful only through validation, human action and repeated follow-up.

## Content rules

- Use plain language.
- State facts only when evidence supports them.
- Mark all recommendations as pilot choices.
- Mark the fictional account example as illustrative.
- Do not use Databricks.
- Do not describe a comment as intent.
- Do not claim a single email creates a conversion.
- Do not use product-marketing language.
- Do not include a final CTA.
- Do not include "THE ASK".
- Do not include the removed "What would make this strategy commercially useful" section or its table.
- Do not include the old Databricks walkthrough.
- Avoid marketing slogans, vague executive language and repeated summaries.

## Acceptance criteria

- The page reads in one linear vertical flow at desktop and mobile widths.
- The complete process includes Clay, CRM, SDR calling, email, LinkedIn, qualification, handoff, nurture and feedback.
- All three supplied screenshots are uncropped.
- One real comment is interpreted without inventing buying intent.
- The worked account example is fictional and clearly labelled.
- Clay's role is technically accurate and evidence based.
- AWS and Todyl claims match their published case studies.
- Multi-touch outreach is the operating model.
- No conversion forecast is invented.
- No Databricks reference remains.
- Source links are included in the research appendix.
